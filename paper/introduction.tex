\section{Introduction}
Neural receivers (NRXs) replace channel estimation, equalization, and demapping with a single neural network trained on data~\cite{honkala2021deeprx}. \citet{cammerer2023neural} brought this concept to multi-user multiple-input multiple-output (MU-MIMO) transmission over the 5G NR physical uplink shared channel (PUSCH), using convolutional and graph neural network (CGNN) layers that refine a state per user over several iterations. More iterations yield more reliable log-likelihood ratios (LLRs), but each iteration also adds latency. In the real-time version of this NRX, a 1\,ms budget admits two of its eight iterations~\cite{wiesmayr2025realtime}. On our evaluation sequences, this cut raises the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) required for a transport block error rate (TBLER) of 10\% by up to 1\,dB, even when the NRX is retrained for two iterations.

Like other NRXs, this receiver processes each slot independently. Yet the channel of a user evolves continuously, and its demodulation reference signals (DMRS) are transmitted in every slot. A stateless NRX therefore discards the pilot evidence that earlier slots have already gathered about the current channel. Classical receivers exploit this temporal correlation by filtering pilots across time or by bundling the DMRS of several slots~\cite{hoeher1997wiener,3gpp38214}. This raises the question of whether stored pilot evidence can substitute for NRX iterations.

In this work, we propose a stateful NRX that carries pilot evidence across slots. It runs two CGNN iterations and keeps, for each user, a memory of least-squares (LS) channel estimates at the DMRS positions of up to seven previous transport blocks (TBs). A small learned channel filter combines these entries with the current pilots and estimates the error covariance of the refined estimate. This covariance enters one round of iterative detection and decoding (IDD), which further compensates for the fewer iterations. The memory is written from pilots only, so it needs no labels and decoding failures cannot corrupt later slots, and all weights stay fixed during operation. We train the receiver end-to-end on sequences of TBs rather than independent slots, starting from the eight-iteration NRX, and add only 643 weights.

We evaluate the proposed NRX in simulations of $2\times 4$ MU-MIMO with 16-QAM over sequences of 64 slots. With two iterations, it performs close to the eight-iteration NRX for urban microcell (UMi) channels, channel changes, and scheduling gaps, and it clearly outperforms the same NRX retrained with two iterations on the same data. The memory alone reduces the required SNR in all three conditions, and its gain grows with the Doppler shift. Without IDD, which leaves the low-density parity-check (LDPC) decoder unchanged, the memory gain remains, and the NRX matches the three-iteration NRX, with less than 3\% more inference time than the two-iteration NRX when its filter runs as one CUDA kernel, whereas IDD is not yet real-time capable. We also use the receiver's evidence for two network decisions. Falling back to a conventional receiver after a cyclic redundancy check (CRC) failure rarely helps, as most errors stem from low channel power. With closed-loop power control, the proposed NRX even outperforms the released eight-iteration NRX, and combining the CRC history with the receiver's evidence lets the network request most retransmissions before decoding finishes, clearly more than the CRC history alone. All results are obtained in simulation with a single training seed.
