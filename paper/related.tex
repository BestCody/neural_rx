\section{Related Work}
On an NVIDIA A100 GPU with TensorRT, the latency of the NRX of
\citet{cammerer2023neural} grows linearly with the number of
iterations~\cite{wiesmayr2025realtime}. Several remedies
recover part of the accuracy lost to fewer iterations. Site-specific fine-tuning lets a
two-iteration receiver outperform an untuned eight-iteration receiver on
single-user over-the-air recordings at two indoor sites~\cite{baytekin2026site},
but it requires recorded site data and degrades performance on the original
training distribution~\cite{wiesmayr2025realtime}. Other work reduces per-slot
compute or model size through dynamic depth and expert
selection~\cite{yang2022dynamic,vanbolderik2024layerskip,vanbolderik2024mean},
low-rank adapters~\cite{vanbolderik2026loren}, weight
quantization~\cite{yellapragada2026qat}, factorized or shallow
attention~\cite{yellapragada2026axial,kawai2025unified,fan2025choose}, lighter or
phase-equivariant blocks~\cite{gupta2025lowcomplexity,gansekoele2025rpe}, and
model-driven hybrids~\cite{honkala2026eqdeeprx,abdollahpour2025mdx}, some at a
cost in accuracy. Yet all of these methods
process each slot from scratch, a statelessness that \citet{kawai2025unified} name as a limitation, calling for temporal
context across resource grids.

Pooling pilots across time is an established way to improve channel estimates.
Two-dimensional Wiener filtering weights pilots by their time--frequency
correlation~\cite{hoeher1997wiener}, Kalman tracking propagates an estimate and
its error covariance between blocks~\cite{komninakis2002kalman}, and 5G NR
Release~17 standardizes joint channel estimation over several PUSCH slots as DMRS
bundling~\cite{3gpp38830,3gpp38214}. Bundling requires a channel that stays correlated over the
window and a continuous transmit phase. Their weights come from channel
statistics or a channel model that a BS must estimate per user, and they stay
the same when a user's channel changes. Learned
predictors exploit the same temporal structure with recurrent, state-space and
transformer models over past channel
estimates~\cite{mattu2022deep,mohsin2025continual,luo2025cpmamba,lagona2026transformer,gizzini2021talstm},
and a hypernetwork can set the parameters of a Kalman
tracker~\cite{pratik2021hkf}. These works, however, target channel prediction or
tracking, and none conditions a multi-user NRX on stored pilot history.
Our filter instead fits its weights per user by least squares on the
stored pilots, like an adaptive filter~\cite{haykin2002adaptive}, chooses on the
current slot how many entries to use, and feeds an NRX. In our evaluation, a
classical multi-slot estimator given each condition's statistics falls 0.56\,dB
behind its single-slot version after a channel change, while our filter stays
ahead of both.

Within learned receivers, earlier slots enter mainly through the weights.
Online schemes retrain on decoder-recovered labels, on symbol decisions, or when
a pilot-based drift test
fires~\cite{fischer2022adaptive,finish2022symbol,uzlaner2025asynchronous}, or
track a posterior over the weights~\cite{gusakov2026online}. They pay in label
delay~\cite{fischer2022adaptive}, in compute that
grows at least quadratically with the number of weights~\cite{gusakov2026online},
and in error propagation when their own decisions serve as labels or references~\cite{fischer2022adaptive,fan2025defined}. Long-term statistics such
as time-averaged channel covariances~\cite{liu2026universal,wiesmayr2026impact}
remain valid across scheduling gaps but adapt slowly after a user change and do
not supply the instantaneous channel. Closest to our setting, \citet{kunde2025transformers} show that a fixed-weight attention layer over past pilot pairs can represent the symbol posterior and empirically outperforms
detectors that treat a linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE) channel estimate
as exact, but only with asymptotic theory and single-user, uncoded experiments.

Despite these advances, no prior MU-MIMO NRX for 5G NR carries
channel estimates across slots to recover the accuracy lost to fewer
iterations. Unlike online adaptation, our receiver keeps its trained weights fixed and
stores only LS channel estimates, so it
needs no labels and no decoder feedback. Unlike DMRS bundling, it requires neither power
consistency nor phase continuity across the stored slots.
