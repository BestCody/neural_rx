\section{Related Work}
Neural receivers for 5G NR obtain their accuracy from iterative inference over
the resource grid, yet the uplink latency budget limits how many iterations can
run. Prior work addresses this tension by making each slot cheaper to process
or by adapting the receiver to its environment, while a separate line of work
exploits the temporal correlation of the channel. Uncertainty-aware detection
and iterative decoding complete the picture, as they determine how much a
receiver can gain from better channel evidence.

Fully learned receivers replace channel estimation, equalization and demapping
with a single network that processes one slot~\cite{honkala2021deeprx}. The
convolutional graph neural network (CGNN) receiver of \citet{cammerer2023neural} extends this to 5G NR multi-user MIMO through
an unrolled iterative algorithm, in which each learned iteration exchanges
messages between users and updates a per-user state. Its latency grows with
depth: on an NVIDIA A100 with TensorRT, each iteration adds about 350~$\mu$s to
a fixed 270~$\mu$s, so a 1~ms budget admits two iterations instead of
eight~\cite{wiesmayr2025realtime}. The reported SNR loss of this cut is below
0.7~dB on a two-user 16-QAM test channel and larger at 64-QAM; on our
evaluation sequences it reaches 1.13--1.53~dB at 10\% TBLER.

Several remedies recover part of this loss. Site-specific finetuning lets a
two-iteration receiver outperform an untuned eight-iteration receiver on
single-user over-the-air recordings at two indoor sites~\cite{baytekin2026site},
but it requires recorded site data and degrades performance on the original
training distribution~\cite{wiesmayr2025realtime}. Other work reduces per-slot
compute or model size through dynamic depth and expert
selection~\cite{yang2022dynamic,vanbolderik2024layerskip,vanbolderik2024mean},
low-rank adapters~\cite{vanbolderik2026loren}, weight
quantization~\cite{yellapragada2026qat}, factorized or shallow
attention~\cite{yellapragada2026axial,kawai2025unified,fan2025choose}, lighter or
phase-equivariant blocks~\cite{gupta2025lowcomplexity,gansekoele2025rpe}, and
model-driven hybrids~\cite{honkala2026eqdeeprx,abdollahpour2025mdx}. Some of these
levers cost accuracy, for example 0.73--0.80~dB for 4-bit
weights~\cite{yellapragada2026qat}, and their costs are reported in units that
cannot be compared directly: FLOPs~\cite{abdollahpour2025mdx,yellapragada2026axial,honkala2026eqdeeprx},
active layers~\cite{vanbolderik2024mean}, modelled
energy~\cite{gupta2025lowcomplexity}, synthesized power and
area~\cite{vanbolderik2024layerskip,vanbolderik2026loren}, and measured
latency~\cite{wiesmayr2025realtime,kawai2025unified}. Yet all of these methods
process each slot from scratch, a statelessness that \citet{kawai2025unified} name as a limitation, calling for temporal
context across resource grids.

Pooling pilots across time is an established way to improve channel estimates.
Two-dimensional Wiener filtering weights pilots by their time--frequency
correlation~\cite{hoeher1997wiener}, Kalman tracking propagates an estimate and
its error covariance between blocks~\cite{komninakis2002kalman}, and 5G NR
Release~17 standardizes joint estimation over several PUSCH slots as DMRS
bundling~\cite{3gpp38830,3gpp38214}, with gains that depend on the number of
bundled slots. These gains require a channel that stays correlated over the
window and a UE whose transmit phase stays continuous. Under isotropic
scattering, the correlation of samples one 0.5~ms slot apart falls from 0.99 at
a Doppler of 50~Hz to 0.64 at 400~Hz~\cite{jakes1974microwave}. Learned
predictors exploit the same temporal structure with recurrent, state-space and
transformer models over past channel
estimates~\cite{mattu2022deep,mohsin2025continual,luo2025cpmamba,lagona2026transformer,gizzini2021talstm},
and a hypernetwork can set the parameters of a Kalman
tracker~\cite{pratik2021hkf}. These works, however, target channel prediction or
tracking; none conditions a neural multi-user receiver on stored pilot history.

Within learned receivers, earlier slots enter mainly through the weights.
Online schemes retrain on decoder-recovered labels, on symbol decisions, or when
a pilot-based drift test
fires~\cite{fischer2022adaptive,finish2022symbol,uzlaner2024asynchronous}, or
track a posterior over the weights~\cite{gusakov2026online}. They pay in label
delay, at least 0.46~s per batch in \citet{fischer2022adaptive}; in compute that
grows at least quadratically with the number of weights~\cite{gusakov2026online};
and in error propagation when decisions serve as labels or
references~\cite{fischer2022adaptive,fan2025defined}. Long-term statistics such
as time-averaged channel covariances~\cite{liu2026universal,wiesmayr2026impact}
remain valid across scheduling gaps but adapt slowly after a user change and do
not supply the instantaneous channel. Closest to our setting, \citet{kunde2025transformers} show that a fixed-weight softmax-attention
layer over past pilot pairs can represent the symbol posterior and, empirically,
outperforms detectors that treat an LMMSE channel estimate as exact; their
theory, however, is asymptotic, and their experiments are single-user and
uncoded.

A detector that treats its channel estimate as exact produces overconfident
log-likelihood ratios (LLRs). \citet{goutay2021ml} learn the
covariance of the channel-estimation error and use it twice: in the equalizer
that separates the users, and in the noise variance that sets the confidence of
each LLR. Combined with a learned demapper, their receiver gains 1~dB at a coded
BER of $10^{-3}$ at 0--15~km/h and 3~dB at $10^{-2}$ at 30--45~km/h.
Iterative detection and decoding (IDD) adds decoder feedback at run time.
DUIDD~\cite{wiesmayr2022duidd} learns the parameters of a fixed number of
detection--decoding rounds: it normalizes the signal by a
noise-plus-channel-error covariance assumed independent of the transmitted
symbols, learns how to mix a posteriori LLRs with extrinsic ones, which exclude
the decoder's prior, and keeps decoder messages between rounds. It gains
0.6--1.2~dB at 1\% BLER over classical IDD with the same decoding budget.
Learned detectors complicate this feedback: an approximate GNN posterior keeps
part of the decoder's prior even after it is subtracted, so the prior is counted
twice and the loop can become unstable. \citet{zhou2023ext} counter
this by training on synthetic priors with extrinsic targets and by clipping
decoder LLRs, and \citet{clausius2025joint} observe a similar
mismatch. In these works, however, the error covariance covers only the current
slot's pilots, and whether a neural receiver's LLRs stay extrinsic under decoder
feedback has not been examined.

Despite these advances, no prior 5G NR multi-user MIMO neural receiver carries
pilot-derived channel evidence across slots to recover the accuracy lost to
fewer iterations. In contrast, our receiver keeps its weights fixed and stores
least-squares DMRS estimates from up to seven earlier transport blocks per UE.
It therefore needs no labels and cannot propagate decision errors. Like DMRS
bundling, it assumes phase continuity across stored slots; it ignores entries
older than eight slots, weights the rest by age, and falls back to the current
estimate when the memory does not help. A channel filter trained with a Gaussian
negative log-likelihood supplies an error covariance for this multi-slot
estimate, which enters a symbol-dependent joint-symbol likelihood in one IDD
round with retained decoder state. The approach is complementary to intra-slot
cost reduction and to weight adaptation. Because our timings come from a
non-real-time implementation, we report savings as iteration counts rather than
as latency.
